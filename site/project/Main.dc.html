A language model that lives on a USB stick and needs nothing from outside.

Free. Open source. Offline. Always yours.

Plug it into any Windows machine, double-click, and talk. Nothing you type leaves the computer. Every byte it learned from is listed with its licence. And when it doesn't know something, it says so, which is measured, not promised.

It doesn't bluff.

Ask about something that doesn't exist and it tells you it has no record of it. Ask something it should know and it answers. Both rates are published, side by side, on a frozen test anyone can rerun.

Every byte is accounted for.

One file lists every source it was trained on: name, licence or public-domain basis, token count, and the hash of the processed slice. The big labs cannot publish that file. This one ships with it.

Nothing leaves your machine.

No account, no telemetry, no update check. Pull the cable and nothing changes. An audit script proves it makes zero network calls, and the threat model says plainly what that does and does not protect you from.

The numbers we publish, always together

A low bluff rate means nothing on its own: a model that says nothing never bluffs. So two numbers are always shown side by side. How often it invents an answer to a question about something that does not exist, and how often it answers a question it should be able to answer.

Thirty invented questions, thirty real ones, twenty-eight contested ones, forty tool-use ones. The test files are in the repository. Run them yourself.

The ledger is the product

Educational web text, permissively licensed code, Wikipedia, Stack Exchange, and the public-domain canon of political economy: Locke, Smith, Bastiat, Mill, Ricardo, Tocqueville, the Federalist Papers. Nothing scraped in secret, nothing from behind a paywall, nothing without a nameable licence. When the rights were unclear, the answer was no.

The file is enough to rebuild the corpus. That is rarer than open weights.

Three switches

OFFLINE or ONLINE. SAND (nothing saved) or STONE. READ-ONLY or CAN ACT. Always visible, always yours to flip.

A memory you can see

A bar shows how full the model's window is. When it fills, the oldest turn is shown leaving, with its first words. It never forgets in secret.

Tools, sandboxed

A calculator, the clock, search over reference packs, reading a file you name, saving a note. Never a shell. Every call is printed before it runs.

Packs, not promises

Things that should be quoted, not remembered, ship as searchable public-domain text: the canon, a survival manual. Drop in your own.

What it isn't, and why you'd want it anyway

It is roughly a thousandth the size of the models you may have used. It knows less. It will not write your novel or debug your kernel. What it will do is tell you when it doesn't know, show you what it was trained on, and keep working on a machine with no network for as long as the stick lasts.

It is released as a finished artifact: no updates, no support, no account. The code is Apache 2.0, the weights CC BY-SA 4.0, and the whole build, mistakes included, is written down. Someone with time, skill, or money can make it bigger. That is the point.
