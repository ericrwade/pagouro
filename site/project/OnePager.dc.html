A language model on a USB stick. Free, open source, offline, always yours.

Built from random weights by one person on a desk computer and a rented GPU, on a corpus where every byte has a nameable licence. It is small on purpose. Its trick is knowing what it doesn't know.

Doesn't bluff

Trained to say "I have no record of that" instead of inventing one. The rate is measured on a frozen public test, next to how often it answers real questions.

Every byte on the ledger

One file: every source, its licence or public-domain basis, token count, and hash. Enough to rebuild the corpus. The big labs cannot publish this file.

Nothing leaves the machine

No account, no telemetry, no update check. Audited to make zero network calls. The threat model states what that does and does not protect.

How it's measured

Four frozen sets: 30 questions about things that don't exist, 30 that a well-read person can answer, 28 contested economic questions, 40 tool-use decisions. Two numbers always published together, because a model that says nothing never bluffs. Baselines on the same test: small open models invent an answer 50–57% of the time; frontier models 23–27%, while answering 97% of the real ones.

Release gate
It ships only if it answers ≥80% of the real questions and its bluff rate is below every open baseline.
Both numbers are printed on the box either way.
Who it's for

People who want to ask a machine anything without a record existing anywhere. People with no connection. Researchers who need a model whose training data can be inspected. Tinkerers who want a complete, documented, small model to make bigger.

What it costs

Nothing. Apache 2.0 code, CC BY-SA 4.0 weights, a signed manifest whose hash is anchored to Bitcoin. A tip-jar address is in the manifesto; nobody is tracking whether you used it. Released as a finished artifact: no updates, no support, fork it.

Where it stands

The full pipeline runs end to end and a proof-of-concept model lives on a stick inside the real application: three switches, a visible memory gauge, five sandboxed tools, reference packs. A 126M-parameter model is training on a rented GPU; the 1B product model follows once its data and gate are ready. The whole build, mistakes included, is a public log at release.

Greek págouros, hermit crab: carries a home it can leave.
