## The resources behind it, checked, and whether each helps

### 1. nanochat (Karpathy, MIT) — yes, as a reference implementation and a benchmark for our efficiency
Checked at github.com/karpathy/nanochat: "train your own GPT-2 capability LLM … for only $48
(~2 hours of 8XH100 GPU node)", spot instances "closer to ~$15"; full pipeline (tokenizer →
pretrain → midtrain/SFT → RL → inference), FineWeb for pretraining, SmolTalk for chat data,
the DCLM CORE score as its metric, MIT licence.

What it can do for Pagouro:
- **Cost sanity check for the 1B run.** Our measured MFU is 15–20% (plain PyTorch, SDPA,
  bf16). nanochat's speedrun reaches GPT-2-class capability in ~1.5–2 h on 8×H100; our
  `docs/JOB_1B.md` budgets 500–700 H100-hours for 1B/100B tokens. If their loop runs at 2–3×
  our MFU (Muon optimizer, compiled kernels, better data loading — to be measured, not
  assumed), the 1B run could cost hundreds, not thousands. **Action:** run nanochat's
  pretraining loop on the A40 for ten minutes against our own `train.py` on the same data and
  compare tokens/s (about $0.10). If it wins clearly, borrow the specific pieces into
  `train.py` rather than replacing it (our checkpoint/resume/DDP/ledger discipline stays).
- **Vocab-invariant validation.** nanochat reports validation loss in bits-per-byte, which is
  comparable across tokenizers. Our D-60 fix made the val set representative; bits-per-byte
  would make it comparable to published models too. Small change to `score_heldout.py`.
- **CORE score.** A standard downstream metric would let the box say "GPT-2-class" or not in
  a number strangers recognise, beside our own four columns. Worth adding at 1B, not before.
- **What we cannot take:** SmolTalk as-is for SFT. It is Apache-2.0 and generated by open
  models, so it is licence-clean, but it is 2024 synthetic chat that teaches general
  assistant behaviour, not abstention. Mixing it in blind would likely *raise* the bluff
  rate. **Possible experiment at Flash scale:** a filtered slice (short factual Q&A only) +
  our harness set, measured on the frozen suite — one CPU afternoon, numbers either way.

### 2. Hugging Face's Smol Training Playbook — yes, read before the 1B mixture is fixed
The author cites it for "real ablation logs". Its documented decay-phase design (upsampling
high-quality and domain data in the final phase) is the published version of our D-9/D-48
choice. Before `build_mixture.py` runs for the 1B volume, its mixture ratios and the
ablations behind them should be read against `JOB_1B.md`'s table. No code, one reading.

### 3. GRPO — the most interesting idea here, and it lands on our headline metric
The post's thread frames SFT as "what to say" and reinforcement fine-tuning (GRPO) as "how to
succeed". Our headline metric is a *policy* property: answer when you can, abstain when you
can't. That is a verifiable reward, and we can generate it programmatically (O-18): pair a
real question with a checkable answer against an invented entity with none, reward +1 for a
correct answer on the real one, +1 for an abstention on the invented one, −1 for a bluff, and
−1 for abstaining on the real one. The paired reward is what stops the obvious hack (abstain
on everything). Runs on a small GPU at 126M–1B; the post's own claim is "5 GB VRAM" for the
explainers it links.

This is not a replacement for SFT; it is a stage after it, and it would be the first time the
bluff rate is *optimised* rather than imitated. **Proposed as O-25:** a GRPO stage on the Flash
model after its SFT, with the reward set built by `scripts/` from held-out facts and generated
non-entities, evaluated on the frozen suite before/after. One session of work plus ~$1 of A40.
Risk to watch: reward hacking via hedge phrasings that satisfy the abstention marker without
being honest — the eval's HEDGE class already catches degenerate output, and the reward must
use the same scorer as the eval or the two will drift.

### 4. Stanford CS336 (Language Modeling from Scratch) — for the book, not the code
Tokenizers, kernels, parallelism, scaling laws, data filtering, alignment, all from scratch.
It is the academic counterpart of what the book does as a diary. It goes in the further-
reading appendix, and its scaling-law lecture is the check on D-6's "1B at 100 tokens per
parameter" before the launch.

## Verdict

The post is a signpost, not a source; its article is unreadable from here. But the signposts
point somewhere real, and two of them change what we should do next:

1. **Measure nanochat's loop against ours on the A40 (ten minutes, ~$0.10)** before the 1B
   budget is trusted. If our loop is the slow one, the 1B run gets cheaper by borrowing.
2. **GRPO on the no-bluff objective (O-25)** after the Flash SFT — the first chance to train
   the headline number directly instead of hoping imitation delivers it.

Neither requires a decision from Eric to prepare; both need his "go" before GPU time is spent
(D-54). The playbook reading and bits-per-byte are free and go in the queue.
